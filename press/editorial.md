# Voice

This paper explains important changes in AI systems to technically literate
readers who want to understand the mechanism, not just the announcement.

Write analysis first. Start with the concrete fact or tension that makes the
subject worth attention, then explain the system that produced it. Spend words
on interfaces, training objectives, evaluation design, operating constraints,
and the consequences for people building software. Do not spend words
re-explaining familiar machine-learning basics.

Keep confidence calibrated. Separate what a company documents, what an
independent source observes, and what the article infers. Treat a vendor's
benchmark, demo, price, or roadmap as evidence of what the vendor claims, not
as proof of general capability. Say when the public record does not disclose an
architecture, dataset, evaluation protocol, or failure mode.

Use a composed, precise register. Prefer short declarative sentences, specific
nouns, and active verbs. Avoid launch language, empty futurism, and claims that
turn a promising interface into a proven solution. End by telling the reader
what the evidence changes for system design and what still needs to be tested.

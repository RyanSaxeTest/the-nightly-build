Dispatches are commissioned technical investigations of an AI system, model,
paper, product, or protocol. Answer the question underneath the request rather
than summarizing its launch. For a new model, establish what the public record
shows about its interface, mechanism, training objective, evaluation, and
operating constraints, then state what remains unknown.

Treat a company announcement as a primary source for what the company says,
not as independent proof that the system works as advertised. Use primary
documentation, papers, source code, or technical results for mechanism. Add
independent reporting or testing when it changes the reader's understanding of
performance, availability, or practical use. Mark synthesis and inference as
such.

Explain how the system fits into real software and where it does not. Do not
turn a typed interface, a demo, or a cost claim into a claim of general
intelligence. Do not invent undisclosed architecture, training data, or
benchmarks. A strong dispatch leaves the reader with a usable model of the
system and a short list of tests that would change the conclusion.

Whatever the request supplied, a link, a document, or a claim, is starting
material rather than the complete evidence. Read it, then read past it until
the piece stands on sources the reader can check. Cover what was asked and
nothing that was not.

# Evidence: news-brief/2026-09-19 (researcher 01)

Two of the three commissioned leads hold up against their own primary
sources with a genuinely independent secondary account: NASA's SpaceX
contract modification (Crew-15/16/17, $946 million, primary NASA release
plus an independent Teslarati account with its own quote and context) and
Nscale's NYSE S-1 filing, whose headline figures I confirmed by pulling the
filing's own text directly rather than trusting search summaries — revenue,
net loss, remaining-performance-obligations, and customer-concentration
figures all check out, with one correction: the $56.4 billion RPO figure and
the $103.4 billion order-book figure carry different as-of dates in the
filing itself (June 30 vs. August 31, 2026), which some aggregated coverage
blurs into one date. The third lead, Anthropic and OpenAI's smaller
data-center deals, does not clear the series' source floor: every account I
could find, including several that explicitly say so in their own text,
traces to one CNBC story built on four anonymous sources, and neither lab
confirmed specifics (OpenAI gave a non-denial; Anthropic declined comment).
There is no primary and no independent secondary for this claim, only one
secondary account repeated verbatim elsewhere. I searched for a fourth
qualifying item dated 2026-09-18/19 and found one strong candidate — Judge
Brinkema's unsealed Google ad-tech remedies opinion — but its unsealing
falls on September 16-17, outside the commissioned window, so I did not
substitute it. That leaves two leads fully verified and one that the writer
and editor need to decide about; see Contradictions and Discarded below.

## Contradictions

- CNBC's own SpaceX headline reads "$950 million," while CNBC's own article body, NASA's release, and Teslarati all state $946 million. This looks like headline rounding rather than a substantive dispute, but the writer should use $946 million (the figure every source, including CNBC's own text, converges on) and not the CNBC headline figure.
- CNBC's SpaceX piece reports SpaceX "is reportedly winding down its Dragon program," while Teslarati quotes SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell saying SpaceX "is not retiring Crew Dragon today, for sure" while declining to commit past 2030. These are in tension: one frames Dragon's retirement as already underway, the other has SpaceX's own president declining to confirm any retirement timeline. The safer framing is Shotwell's own on-record statement.
- Nscale's S-1 discloses two different scale metrics that read similarly but are not the same figure: remaining performance obligations of $56.4 billion is dated to June 30, 2026 (the financial-statement period end); active-and-contracted total contract value of $103.4 billion is dated separately to August 31, 2026 (a later operational disclosure, alongside $2.6 billion "active" TCV). I could not find a secondary account that clearly preserves this distinction — most compress it into a single "as of August" figure. The writer should attribute each figure to its own date rather than treating them as one number.
- The Anthropic-Nscale deal is commonly described in press coverage (including CNBC's own framing of it as context for lead 3) as "roughly $45 billion." The S-1 itself states "up to approximately $44.6 billion," explicitly as a ceiling contingent on delivery and service-availability milestones, and separately discloses that Nscale has not yet secured the financing needed to build out its side of the deal. The $44.6 billion figure, with its "up to" qualifier, is the more precise and defensible number.
- Lead 3 itself is a contradiction of the commission's framing: the commission describes it as something "reported... per reporting from around 2026-09-18," but on inspection there is exactly one substantive account (CNBC), not multiple independent ones. Every other piece I opened either explicitly cites CNBC as its source or is a close paraphrase of it. Neither Anthropic nor OpenAI confirmed the specific 20-30 MW figure, the UK/Nordic locations, or that any site has been chosen.

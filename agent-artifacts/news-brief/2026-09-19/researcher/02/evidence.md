# Evidence: news-brief/2026-09-19 (researcher 02)

This record preserves round 01's two fully verified items without re-research —
NASA's SpaceX contract modification and Nscale's NYSE S-1 filing — and adds two
new items that independently clear the series' source floor: Infillion's
acquisition of Foursquare and EQT Infrastructure's $2 billion backing of
Madison Energy Infrastructure's distributed-battery buildout for AI data
centers, both announced September 18, 2026. That brings the commission to four
verified, distinct items, meeting the brief template's floor without padding.
I looked hard at the round 01 candidate it flagged but did not use — Judge
Brinkema's unsealed Google ad-tech remedies opinion — for a fresh 2026-09-18/19
hook, and found none: the market reaction (Magnite up as much as 12.9%,
PubMatic up roughly 6%) and every follow-on account I could locate trace to the
opinion's September 15-16 unsealing, not to a new date-appropriate development,
so I left it out, as round 01 did. I also fully verified a strong fifth
candidate — the FDA's September 18, 2026 approval of Aqneursa (levacetylleucine)
as the first-ever treatment for ataxia-telangiectasia — but did not select it as
one of my two new items, because it is a health/biotech development rather than
a technology-industry one, and the two items I did select sit closer to the
series' actual ground (ad-tech consolidation, AI data-center power
infrastructure). It is fully recorded in Discarded below in case the writer or
editor wants a fifth item instead of one of my picks. Two other promising leads
did not clear the window or the citation bar on inspection: a Department of
Energy quantum-computing funding competition (its own page carries a September
17 date stamp, one day outside the window, the same problem round 01 found with
the Brinkema opinion) and an FCC conditional-approval listing for a robotics
importer (the only FCC document I could actually open is dated September 9,
and does not name the company in question; the September 18 date comes only
from that company's own press release, which I could not independently
confirm against an FCC record). I also ruled out California Governor Newsom's
September 18 AI executive order, which clears the window cleanly but is
squarely AI safety-testing and model-misalignment territory — a proposed "kill
switch" and independent frontier-safety oversight, explicitly framed across
every account of it as a response to the same runaway-AI fears behind the
Gemini and OpenAI stories — that the commission reserved for the feature
article; I did not open its primary text myself and am not treating it as a
verified source, only as a topic-boundary judgment call based on consistent
independent reporting of its content.

Where the new evidence is thin: the Infillion/Foursquare deal terms (purchase
price, closing date) are undisclosed by design, so the item rests on the fact
of the acquisition and its stated scale rather than a price tag. The EQT/Madison
item's $2 billion figure is "up to," phased through 2028, and contingent on
future project development, not a signed, fully committed capital deployment —
the writer should frame it as a target, not a completed spend.

## Contradictions

- CNBC's own SpaceX headline reads "$950 million," while CNBC's own article body, NASA's release, and Teslarati all state $946 million. This looks like headline rounding rather than a substantive dispute, but the writer should use $946 million (the figure every source, including CNBC's own text, converges on) and not the CNBC headline figure.
- CNBC's SpaceX piece reports SpaceX "is reportedly winding down its Dragon program," while Teslarati quotes SpaceX President Gwynne Shotwell saying SpaceX "is not retiring Crew Dragon today, for sure" while declining to commit past 2030. These are in tension: one frames Dragon's retirement as already underway, the other has SpaceX's own president declining to confirm any retirement timeline. The safer framing is Shotwell's own on-record statement.
- Nscale's S-1 discloses two different scale metrics that read similarly but are not the same figure: remaining performance obligations of $56.4 billion is dated to June 30, 2026 (the financial-statement period end); active-and-contracted total contract value of $103.4 billion is dated separately to August 31, 2026 (a later operational disclosure, alongside $2.6 billion "active" TCV). I could not find a secondary account that clearly preserves this distinction — most compress it into a single "as of August" figure. The writer should attribute each figure to its own date rather than treating them as one number.
- The Anthropic-Nscale deal is commonly described in press coverage as "roughly $45 billion." The S-1 itself states "up to approximately $44.6 billion," explicitly as a ceiling contingent on delivery and service-availability milestones, and separately discloses that Nscale has not yet secured the financing needed to build out its side of the deal. The $44.6 billion figure, with its "up to" qualifier, is the more precise and defensible number.
- Madison Energy Infrastructure's own release states two different "1 gigawatt" figures on two different timelines: its existing operating portfolio is expected to itself exceed 1 GW by fall 2026 (a figure about Madison's pre-existing business), while the new "Community Infrastructure" initiative separately targets 1 additional GW of new capacity by 2028 (backed by the $2 billion). Neither Bloomberg nor CryptoBriefing flagged this distinction explicitly; a careless read could conflate the two into one number, which the writer should not do.
- No contradiction found between the Infillion, Axios, and AdExchanger accounts of the Foursquare acquisition. The 150/650 employee counts and the "20% to 25%" revenue/headcount projection appear only in Axios's interview with Emrich, not in Infillion's own written release; this is a gap in the primary document, not a disagreement between sources, and the writer should attribute those specific figures to Axios's reporting rather than to Infillion's own announcement.

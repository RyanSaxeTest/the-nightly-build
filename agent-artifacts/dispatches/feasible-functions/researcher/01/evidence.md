# Evidence record: dispatches/feasible-functions

## Working answer

Constrained Neural Parameterization (CNP) changes the optimization variable, not merely the loss. Instead of letting a neural network return an arbitrary function and charging it for violations, it composes the network with a map whose image is inside the admissible set. For polyhedral sets in a Hilbert space, the map is the sum of (i) a projection of the network into the orthogonal complement of the constraint normals, (ii) a convex combination of feasible vertices, and (iii) a nonnegative combination of feasible extreme rays. For pointwise constraints, squares, `sin^2`, and recursive stick-breaking play the same role. For selected PDE-constrained problems, the control is eliminated as `u = G(y)`, so a constrained state parameterization makes the PDE identity exact at evaluation points.

The guarantee stops at the image and, under assumptions, the density of the ansatz family. It does not prove that finite-width gradient training finds a global optimum. The paper itself leaves convergence analysis for gradient-based optimization open. The exact-reduced PDE construction also needs a separable operator relation and enough state regularity to evaluate that operator. In the complementarity experiment, nonnegativity and the PDE are hard-coded but orthogonality is still penalized.

## Focal source: paper

- **Source:** Michael Hintermüller and Jianfeng Ning, “Constrained Neural Parameterization for Optimization in Function Spaces,” arXiv:2606.00855, v1, 30 May 2026. Primary source. [Abstract](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.00855), [PDF](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.00855).
- **Locator — abstract and §1, pp. 1–3:** frames function-space optimization with inequality, pointwise, boundary, and PDE constraints; contrasts penalty, projection, primal-dual/KKT, and multiplier-based neural formulations; states the paper's contribution as smooth parameterizations with image inside the feasible set and dense in it under assumptions.
- **Locator — Theorem 2.3, §2.1, pp. 4–5, Eq. (2.9)–(2.14):** for `P = {x in X | <alpha_i,x> <= b_i}`, with `V_alpha = span{alpha_i}`, gives `P = V_alpha^perp + conv({y_i}) + cone({z_i})`. The finite-dimensional part is pointed; `y_i` and `z_i` are vertices and extreme rays. The paper explains that these can be computed from active constraint sets once the number of constraints is moderate.
- **Locator — Condition 2.1, §2.2, p. 5, Eq. (2.15)–(2.18):** requires differentiable coefficient maps; the `g_i` image dense in the probability simplex; and each `h_i` range dense in `[0,infinity)`. Softmax gives strictly positive simplex coefficients and therefore misses the boundary; the recursive `sin^2` map reaches the entire simplex. Representative ray maps are `h_i(gamma_i) = gamma_i^2` or `exp(gamma_i)`.
- **Locator — Eq. (2.19), Lemma 2.2, pp. 5–6:** with an orthonormal basis `alpha_hat_i` of `V_alpha`, defines `U = N - sum <N,alpha_hat_i> alpha_hat_i + sum g_i y_i + sum h_i z_i`. The first part is in `V_alpha^perp`; the convex part is feasible; the ray part preserves every inequality. Lemma 2.2 states feasibility for every parameter value and density in `P` if the unconstrained network is a universal approximator and the scalar maps meet Condition 2.1.
- **Locator — §2.3, pp. 6–10, Eq. (2.26)–(2.40):** treats constraint functionals in a larger space and Dirichlet boundary conditions. It introduces biorthogonal correction directions when the constraint functionals do not live in the network's native space, and uses a boundary-lifting function `w` with homogeneous network factors to enforce zero boundary data.
- **Locator — §3, pp. 10–11, Eq. (3.1)–(3.6):** gives pointwise parameterizations: lower bound `u = phi + N^2`; bounded interval `u = phi_1 + sin^2(N)(phi_2 - phi_1)`; and the recursive multi-phase/Gibbs-simplex construction `u_1 = sin^2(N_1)`, `u_j = (1 - sum_{i<j} u_i) sin^2(N_j)`, with the final component closing the sum to one. Dirichlet conditions are added with a boundary factor.
- **Locator — §4.1, pp. 11–13, Eq. (4.1)–(4.5):** assumes a PDE relation `u = G(y)` and reduces `min J(y,u)` subject to `E(y,u)=0` to `min J(y,G(y))` over admissible states. For `-Delta y = f + u`, `y <= 1`, `y in H_0^1`, it uses `y = 1 - (N w + 1)^2` and `u = -Delta y - f`; automatic differentiation supplies derivatives and the PDE is satisfied at each evaluation point without a residual penalty.
- **Locator — §4.1, pp. 12–13 and Remark 4.1:** the approximation argument needs `G` continuous from an appropriate `H^k` state space to the control space and a CNP ansatz dense in that state space. For Poisson, `H^2(Omega) cap H_0^1(Omega)` plus `f in L^2` is sufficient for `G(y) = -Delta y - f` to lie in `L^2`. The paper warns that weakly feasible states on nonsmooth/nonconvex domains need not have this regularity, and that eliminating the state rather than the control is more general but makes state constraints problematic in general.
- **Locator — §4.2, pp. 13–14, Eq. (4.6)–(4.9):** for polygonal domains with re-entrant corners, decomposes the state as `y = R + S`, with `S = sum gamma_j eta_rho(r_j) s_j`, `s_j = r_j^(lambda_j) sin(lambda_j theta_j)`, `lambda_j = pi/omega_j`; the network learns the regular part and the singular coefficients are trainable, while the control is reconstructed by applying the PDE operator exactly.
- **Locator — §5, pp. 15–22:** all examples use PyTorch, fully connected residual networks with `tanh`, Adam, and automatic differentiation. The paper reports synthetic/reference-solution experiments, not a theorem about arbitrary data distributions.
- **Locator — Example 5.1, p. 15, Table 1:** two integral constraints. CNP relative `L2` errors are `1.90e-3` for `u` and `6.27e-3` for `u'`. Penalty errors for `beta = 10, 100, 1000` are respectively `1.52e-2, 3.29e-3, 2.24e-2` for `u`, and `3.29e-2, 3.80e-2, 1.59e-1` for `u'`. The table supports the paper's claim that this test is more accurate and less sensitive to the penalty choice; it does not establish universal superiority.
- **Locator — Examples 5.2–5.3, pp. 15–17:** the ten-dimensional state-constrained control test compares exact reduction with a KKT-based network and reports lower state/control errors and lower network/memory burden for exact reduction. The low-regularity state-constraint test reports exact-reduced relative errors `6.30e-4` for the state and `1.02e-2` for the control against the primal-dual active-set reference; the KKT attempt with four networks is described as unsatisfactory and omitted.
- **Locator — Example 5.4, pp. 17–18, Eq. (5.12)–(5.16):** complementarity problem with `y >= 0`, `xi >= 0`, and `<y,xi> = 0`. Squared networks enforce nonnegativity; the PDE is exact through reduction; orthogonality is a penalty with `beta = 100`. Reported relative `L2` errors are `1.00e-3` state, `2.07e-2` multiplier, and `1.05e-2` control. This is the clearest boundary on the phrase “hard constraints.”
- **Locator — Example 5.5, p. 20:** Navier–Stokes optimal control; exact-reduced final control error `6.18e-3`, less than half the KKT-based result; the exact-reduced error is about `1.5e-2` after roughly 2,500 epochs and the KKT method costs more time and memory per epoch.
- **Locator — Example 5.6, pp. 20–21, Eq. (5.26)–(5.32):** L-shaped domain. Without singularity enrichment, state/control errors are `2.81e-2` and `4.32e-2`; with the analytic corner mode, they fall to `4.02e-3` and `1.06e-2`; learned singular coefficient `gamma = 0.9997`.
- **Locator — §6, pp. 22–23:** conclusion repeats exact-feasibility and empirical claims and lists open work: rigorous gradient-optimization convergence; variational/non-separable/nonlinear-control extensions; mixed constraints; and operator learning.

## Implementation source

- **Source:** authors' [CNP_Code repository](https://github.com/JianfengNing/CNP_Code). Primary implementation source; README names the paper and authors.
- **Locator — `CNP_Codes/Example_5.1/CNP.py`:** uses a fully connected residual network with `tanh`, two residual blocks, trainable `lambda_param`, Adam with network learning rate `1e-3` and scalar-parameter rate `1e-2`, a step scheduler, and automatic differentiation for `u_x`. The returned `u` is the network output plus analytic correction terms and squared scalar parameters; this is a direct implementation of the paper's integral-constraint construction rather than a separate violation penalty.
- **Locator — `CNP_Codes/Example_5.2/ERNM_CNP.py`:** ten-dimensional state parameterization multiplies the network by a product boundary factor `(1 - x_i^2)`, corrects an integral constraint with a squared scalar parameter, differentiates the state twice to form the control, and trains a 128-wide, three-block `tanh` ResNet.
- **Locator — `CNP_Codes/Example_5.3/state_bound_contraint.py`:** encodes the upper state bound as `y = psi - (N w + sqrt(psi))^2`, computes the control as `-Delta y`, and trains the reduced objective with automatic differentiation. This makes the square construction concrete and shows that the PDE operator is evaluated on the parameterized state.
- **Implementation boundary:** the repository is an example suite, not an independent convergence proof. Its scripts use synthetic exact solutions and example-specific correction formulas, so the article should use the code to explain the mechanics but take guarantees and reported numbers from the paper.

## Related primary and official sources

1. **Boyd and Vandenberghe, _Convex Optimization_.** [Official book page](https://web.stanford.edu/~boyd/cvxbook/). Official reference source. Chapter 2 supplies the standard convex-set/polyhedron vocabulary and finite-dimensional convex-combination framing. It corroborates the geometric language, but it is not evidence for the paper's infinite-dimensional Hilbert-space theorem; that claim belongs to Theorem 2.3 of the focal paper.
2. **Raissi, Perdikaris, and Karniadakis, “Physics-informed neural networks.”** [Journal DOI](https://doi.org/10.1016/j.jcp.2018.10.045). Primary paper. Establishes the residual-based PINN context: neural approximations are trained with differential-equation residual information and data/initial-boundary terms. Use it to explain the contrast between a PDE residual in the loss and the focal paper's algebraic elimination; do not claim every PINN uses the same loss.
3. **Lu et al., “Physics-informed neural networks with hard constraints for inverse design.”** [arXiv:2102.04626](https://arxiv.org/abs/2102.04626). Primary paper. Its abstract explicitly says that the hPINN method imposes “hard constraints” using a penalty method and an augmented Lagrangian method. This is a useful terminology check: “hard” in that paper does not mean the same architectural image-in-set guarantee as CNP.
4. **Dai et al., “Solving Elliptic Optimal Control Problems via Neural Networks and Optimality System.”** [arXiv:2308.11925](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.11925) and [HTML](https://arxiv.org/html/2308.11925v2). Primary paper. Describes a coupled neural approximation of a first-order optimality/KKT system and provides error analysis. Use it as the comparison point for the focal paper's one-network exact-reduced approach and its avoidance of explicit adjoint/multiplier networks.
5. **Hu, Jin, and Zhou, “Solving Poisson Problems in Polygonal Domains with Singularity Enriched Physics Informed Neural Networks.”** [arXiv:2308.16429](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.16429). Primary paper. Its abstract identifies reduced accuracy from limited solution regularity near geometric singularities and explicitly enriches the ansatz with analytic corner/edge behavior. Use it to contextualize the focal paper's `R + S` construction, without treating the two methods as identical.
6. **Barry-Straume et al., “Physics-informed neural networks for PDE-constrained optimization and control.”** [arXiv:2205.03377](https://arxiv.org/abs/2205.03377). Primary paper. Introduces Control PINNs that learn state and control together while encoding optimality conditions in architecture/loss. Use it as adjacent neural PDE-control context, not as evidence that CNP's exact feasibility is standard.
7. **E and Yu, “The Deep Ritz method.”** [arXiv:1710.00211](https://arxiv.org/abs/1710.00211). Primary paper. Variational neural PDE solver context: a deep model minimizes an energy functional with stochastic-gradient training. It supports the article's distinction between optimizing a parameterized functional and enforcing constraints in the parameterization.

## Claims that should be stated carefully

- “Every network output is feasible” is valid only for the constraints encoded by the selected CNP, with the paper's differentiability, geometry, and regularity assumptions. It is not a guarantee about constraints omitted from the map or about numerical floating-point implementation details.
- “Dense in the feasible set” is conditional on the base network's universal approximation property, the scalar maps' density, and the chosen topology. It says the family can approximate admissible functions; it does not say finite training reaches the approximant.
- The softmax variant does not attain zero simplex coefficients. If exact boundary points of the simplex matter, the recursive `sin^2` construction is the paper's stronger option.
- The exact-reduced PDE method is not a generic PDE solver. It requires a usable `u = G(y)` relation and enough differentiability for `G(y)` to belong to the control space. The paper explicitly notes the problem on nonsmooth domains and handles one such case with singularity enrichment.
- “No penalty” needs scope. The main CNP examples remove penalty terms for the encoded polyhedral/pointwise/PDE constraints, but Example 5.4 still penalizes complementarity orthogonality.
- The experiments compare against particular penalty/KKT/primal-dual implementations, widths, sampling schemes, and synthetic references. Report the numbers with their example number and metric; do not generalize them to all neural solvers.
